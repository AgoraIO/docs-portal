## Why Agora

Teams usually adopt Agora when they need to solve more than one problem at the same time:

- Keep voice and video interactions stable under real-world network conditions.
- Synchronize user state, events, and messaging alongside live media.
- Add conversational AI to a live session instead of treating AI as a disconnected backend feature.
- Extend a live product with recording, analytics, whiteboard collaboration, transcription, and operational tooling.

In practice, this means you can start with one entry point, such as voice calling or an AI voice agent, and then expand into adjacent capabilities without rebuilding the underlying session architecture.

## Core product areas

You can understand Agora more clearly by grouping its products into four capability layers instead of reading them as isolated SKUs.

### Realtime interaction

This layer is responsible for the live session itself: who joins, what media flows through the session, and how quickly participants can interact.

- Voice Calling
- Video Calling
- Broadcast Streaming
- Real-Time Engagement for large-scale interactive scenarios
- Device-side realtime connectivity for IoT and embedded hardware

Typical use cases include one-to-one calls, group meetings, live classrooms, social audio rooms, interactive shows, connected devices, and hybrid human-device experiences.

### Realtime coordination

Live products also need metadata and application state to move in real time. Agora covers that layer with messaging and signaling capabilities such as:

- user presence
- channel and topic messaging
- metadata synchronization
- storage and history
- locks and coordination primitives
- event-driven room state updates

This is the layer that keeps your product logic aligned with your media logic.

### Conversational AI

Agora also provides an AI-oriented layer for voice-driven and multimodal experiences. Instead of treating speech input, LLM reasoning, and speech output as separate systems, you can orchestrate them inside the realtime session.

This layer typically includes:

- realtime audio streaming between users and agents
- speech recognition and speech synthesis integration
- LLM orchestration
- interruption handling and turn management
- agent state and lifecycle control
- server-side integration points for business logic

This is the foundation for AI assistants, tutors, copilots, customer service agents, and other voice-first AI products.

### Media services and operations

Once a live experience works, production teams usually need more than the session itself. Agora extends the live path with service and governance capabilities such as:

- cloud and on-premise recording
- media processing and transcoding
- realtime transcription and translation
- whiteboard collaboration
- usage analytics and monitoring
- security controls and production operations

These services help turn a working demo into a production-ready system.

## How the platform fits together

The most useful mental model is to think in layers of responsibility:

1. **Account and project setup** define your access boundary, credentials, billing, and enabled services.
2. **Sessions and channels** define the live context where users, devices, and agents interact.
3. **Media and messaging** carry voice, video, text, metadata, and events through that context.
4. **Server-side control** manages authentication, callbacks, orchestration, business rules, and integrations.
5. **Production services** add analytics, recording, monitoring, compliance, and operational governance.

If you keep this model in mind, the rest of the documentation becomes much easier to navigate. Product pages may focus on different capabilities, but they all connect back to the same live session architecture.
